AI features can make a mobile app feel built for each person who opens it. They can also add cost, delay and risk if they are added for their own sake. The useful ones share a pattern: they remove a specific chore or make a specific decision easier.

This guide covers six AI features we see working in mobile apps, with the questions to answer before you build each one. We build AI into products in three main ways: personalization, task automation and behavior prediction. Most of the examples below fall into one of those groups.

## 1. Personalized content and recommendations

Personalization changes what each user sees based on what they do. A fitness app reorders workouts around what a user completes. A learning app picks the next lesson from the user's mistakes. A marketplace shows listings that match past searches.

**When it helps:** your app has more content or options than one person will browse, and users have different goals.

**What to check:** you need enough usage data to make good suggestions. Start with simple rules, such as "recently used" and "most relevant," and add machine learning once you can measure that suggestions improve results.

## 2. Smart input and task automation

Typing on a phone is slow. AI can reduce it by filling in fields, sorting entries into categories, reading text from photos and turning a short note into a structured record.

Examples include scanning a receipt to create an expense, suggesting a category for a new task and turning a voice note into a checklist.

**When it helps:** your users repeat the same data entry many times a day.

**What to check:** always let users correct the result. A wrong suggestion that is hard to fix frustrates more than no suggestion.

## 3. Behavior prediction and timely nudges

Prediction uses past behavior to estimate what a user is likely to do next. An app can send a reminder at the time a person usually completes a task, flag users who are drifting away or suggest the next action in a routine.

**When it helps:** timing matters, such as habits, appointments, renewals and restocking.

**What to check:** nudges must stay useful. Too many notifications push users to turn them off or delete the app. Give users control over frequency, and measure whether a nudge led to a useful action, not only whether it was opened.

## 4. Natural language search and commands

Users often know what they want but not what the app calls it. Natural language search lets them type "invoices from last month for the Lopez account" instead of building a filter.

**When it helps:** your app holds a lot of structured data, such as records, documents or transactions.

**What to check:** test with real phrases from your audience. Niche users have specialized vocabulary, and a general model may misread it unless you provide the terms.

## 5. Summaries and drafting

AI can summarize long content, such as meeting notes, customer messages or reports, and can draft replies for the user to review. This saves time in apps where people read and write a lot.

**When it helps:** the user's job involves repetitive writing or reading.

**What to check:** keep a human in the loop for anything sent to others. Show the draft, let the user edit and make clear what the app generated.

## 6. A support assistant inside the app

An in-app assistant answers common questions using your own help content. It reduces support load and helps users get unstuck without leaving the app.

**When it helps:** your app has enough features that users regularly ask "how do I...?"

**What to check:** limit the assistant to your documentation and make it easy to reach a person. A confident wrong answer damages trust.

## What to check before adding any AI feature

- **Is the problem real?** Confirm through user research that the chore exists. Our guide to [niche market research](/blog/niche-market-research-for-app-ideas) shows how.
- **Does it need AI?** Some problems are solved by a good default or a simple rule. Use AI where rules cannot cope.
- **How will you handle data and privacy?** Decide what data the feature uses, where it is processed and what users are told. Follow the laws that apply to your audience.
- **What happens when it is wrong?** Plan for errors, with correction paths and fallbacks.
- **What will it cost to run?** AI calls have ongoing costs that grow with usage. Include them in your budget, as described in [what drives app costs](/blog/how-much-does-it-cost-to-build-a-mobile-app).
- **How will you measure it?** Pick one result, such as time saved or tasks completed, and compare before and after.

## Start small

Add one AI feature to a first version that already does its main job well. Measure it, improve it and then decide whether to add another. A focused app with one well-chosen feature beats an app with many half-working ones. ## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need a lot of data to use AI in my app?

Not for every feature. Smart input, summaries and search can work with pre-trained models. Personalization and prediction improve as you collect usage data.

### Will AI features slow down my app?

They can if they run on every screen load. Run them in the background where possible, cache results and show the interface first.

### Is AI worth it for a small niche audience?

Often yes, because a small audience has specific needs that general apps ignore. The key is choosing a feature that solves one of those needs.
