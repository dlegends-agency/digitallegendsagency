App store optimization, or ASO, is the work of making your app easy to find in the App Store and Google Play and easy to choose once someone lands on your page. For a niche app it matters even more than for a mass-market one, because your users search with specific words and you cannot outspend large competitors on ads.

This checklist covers the parts of ASO that you control: keywords, listing text, visuals, ratings and measurement. Stores change their rules and limits over time, so check the current guidelines from Apple and Google before you finalize your listing.

## What ASO does for a niche app

People discover apps in two main ways: they search, or they browse features and charts. Niche apps win through search. Someone who types "route planner for mobile dog groomers" has a clear need, and an app that matches those words can reach them with no advertising.

ASO also improves conversion. A clear listing turns more visitors into installs, which in turn can help your ranking.

## Step 1: Choose keywords your audience actually uses

Start with the language you collected in your [market research](/blog/niche-market-research-for-app-ideas). Write down the phrases people use to describe the problem, the task and the tools they use today.

Then sort them:

- **Specific phrases** that describe your niche and task. These are your best targets, since fewer apps compete for them.
- **Broader category terms** such as "scheduling" or "invoicing." They bring more searches but more competition.
- **Competitor names.** Be careful here. Stores restrict using other brands' names in your listing.

Check the search results for each term. If the first page is full of unrelated apps, the term has little competition and may suit you. If it shows apps for your exact audience, study how they describe themselves.

## Step 2: Write a title and subtitle that match intent

Your app name and the short text beside it carry the most weight in search and are the first thing people read. Put your most important keyword in the name or subtitle and make the benefit clear.

Good listings answer two questions at a glance: what is this, and who is it for? A name followed by a plain description, such as a tool name plus the job it does for the audience, usually beats a clever name alone.

Keep the text readable. Stuffing keywords makes the listing look spammy and can violate store rules.

## Step 3: Use the description to convince, not only to rank

On Google Play, the description is indexed for search, so use your keywords naturally in readable sentences. On the App Store, the description does not affect search ranking the same way, but it still helps people decide.

Write for the visitor:

- Lead with the main benefit in the first lines.
- Describe the tasks your audience does, in their vocabulary.
- Mention the features that matter most, in a short list.
- Say who the app is for. A niche app should state its niche clearly.

## Step 4: Design screenshots and previews that explain the app

Most people decide from the screenshots and the first lines of text. Use the screenshot space to tell a story:

- The first screenshot should show the main benefit and the main screen.
- Add a short caption in the audience's language to each screenshot.
- Show real use, with the interface in a realistic state.
- If a short preview video helps explain the workflow, add one.

Update visuals when your interface changes, so the store page matches what people get.

## Step 5: Earn ratings and reviews at the right moment

Ratings affect both ranking and trust. Ask for a review after a user has completed the main task successfully, not on first launch or in the middle of something. Use the official in-app review prompts that the platforms provide, and respect their limits on how often you can ask.

Reply to reviews, especially critical ones. A calm, helpful reply shows prospective users that someone is looking after the app. Fix the problems that appear repeatedly, because they often point to the next improvement.

## Step 6: Localize if your niche spans regions

If your audience works in several languages or countries, translate the listing, not only the app. Localized titles, keywords and screenshots can open search traffic that competitors ignore. Start with the one or two languages where you already have users.

## Step 7: Measure and improve

Both stores give you data on impressions, page views and installs. Watch three numbers:

1. **Impressions to page views.** Shows whether your title and icon attract clicks in search.
2. **Page views to installs.** Shows whether the page convinces visitors.
3. **Installs to active users.** Shows whether the right people are installing.

Change one thing at a time, such as the first screenshot or the subtitle, and compare results over a few weeks. Both stores offer tools for testing listing variations.

## Go beyond the stores

ASO works best with other channels pointing at your listing. A page on your website that explains the app and links to the stores, articles that answer your audience's questions and posts in the communities where your users gather all send people who are ready to install. Your launch plan from [how to build a niche mobile app](/blog/how-to-build-a-niche-mobile-app) should include these from the start.

## ASO checklist

- [ ] List the phrases your audience uses
- [ ] Check competition for each phrase
- [ ] Put the main keyword in the name or subtitle
- [ ] Write a description that leads with the benefit
- [ ] Add captions to screenshots that show the main task
- [ ] Ask for reviews after a success, and reply to feedback
- [ ] Localize for your top languages
- [ ] Track impressions, page views and installs monthly

## Frequently asked questions

### How long does ASO take to work?

Small changes can show up within days or weeks, but ranking shifts build over time. Review your results monthly rather than daily.

### Do I need paid ads for a niche app?

Not at the start. Search and community channels can bring a small, well-matched audience. Paid ads can help later, once you know your conversion rate and what an install is worth.

### How often should I update my listing?

Review it every time you ship a major feature, and at least a few times a year. Keep keywords, screenshots and text in step with the app.
